# E-000036 — Exactly zero is not absent

**Gating as a deletion primitive, measured at both ends of the gate's range; and the certificate that
cannot see one of them.**

One experiment. Trains nothing. Scores the recorded E-000010 checkpoints and the recorded E-000012
GPT-2 adapter. Two arms, one claim, ~2 CPU-hours idle.

*Renumbered to E-000036 by the author of this repository: E-000035 was taken by the
deletion-disclosure experiment while this design was being written. The design is otherwise as the
agent that produced it wrote it; its central claim was verified before renumbering and the fix it
prescribes is in `so/audit.py` at HEAD (see ledger §31.20).*

---

## 1. The defensible claim, after the prior-art overlaps are removed

**The architecture half is a rediscovery, and I will not build it.** "Stop deriving the address from
the content; materialise the index as an ordinary deletable record" is server-aided MLE (DupLESS)
plus the relational fact that an index is derived data and deletion propagates to it. The attack that
motivates it is convergent encryption's confirmation-of-a-file; the security definition it violates is
*backward privacy* in dynamic SSE; the storage-layer twin is soft-delete in a vector index. Half B is
self-composition for non-interference (Barthe–D'Argenio–Rezk) decided by bounded enumeration of a
finite secret domain — bounded model checking, with two choices of low-observation lattice.

And the decisive point, which settles the design rather than merely qualifying it: **the architectural
remedy is already in this tree and already recorded at chance.** `MVCCStore.evict()` (`so/mvcc.py:221`)
takes the row out of `bank()` while keeping every version, and `so/results/e000028_key_channel.json`
records, on unmodified checkpoints with no retraining, `evict/object_top1 = 0.0040` against a chance of
0.0039, `evict/mean_rank = 128.02` against 127.5, with `evict/payload_retained = 1.0` and
`evict/restore_reads_again = 1.0`. Link-index cells, a `readable_bank()`, or a `content_free` bank
would spend CPU re-deriving a number the repository already has.

What survives is not machinery. It is a statement about a **repair pattern** the field applies
everywhere, and it is ours only because this is the one system where both of its failure modes are
measurable at once:

> **A multiplicative gate controls amplitude. Deletion is a property of support. They are not
> interchangeable, and they come apart in *opposite* ways at the two ends of the gate's range. At
> `g > 0` a residue of the payload-derived quantity survives, and whether that residue is enough is an
> empirical question the repository has never asked. At `g == 0` *exactly* the quantity survives in the
> sign bits, because `x * 0.0` in IEEE-754 is a signed zero and `v_fwd(o) * 0.0` is a bijection onto
> the payload domain. And the certificate this programme built precisely because failed attacks are
> channel-incomplete is itself blind to the second one — its stated claim is bit-identity, its
> comparator implements numerical equality, and exactly-zero multiplication is the operation where
> those two differ.**

I measured both halves on the recorded checkpoints before writing this, so the pre-registration below
is not speculation.

**(a) The gate's shipped repair is real but may not reach chance.** `ModelConfig.gate_reverse_key`
(`so/model.py:52`, `:161`, `:213-219`) blends `k_r = g·k_r + (1-g)·shred_rev_key`. It has never been evaluated;
§31.10 point 4 asserts it needs its own training run. It does not — under `hard_gate` the blend is
`1·k_r + 0·shred` on live rows, bit-exactly the identity — so it is a drop-in on the recorded E-000010
checkpoints. Run at soft gate on `e000010_seed0`, 20 targets, E-000028's own attack code:

| gate | flag | ACTIVE top1 | SHRED top1 | SHRED mean rank | SHRED margin | g on shredded rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| soft (recorded config) | off | 1.0000 | **1.0000** | 0.00 | 0.7044 | mean 8.4e-04, max 5.8e-03 |
| soft | on | 1.0000 | **0.0500** | 109.15 | 3.44e-04 | mean 8.4e-04, max 5.8e-03 |
| **hard** | off | 1.0000 | **1.0000** | 0.00 | 0.7044 | **exactly 0.0** |

The third row is the control that makes the whole thing bite: **the hard gate — the configuration
that earns every certificate in §31.14 — leaves E-000028's attack at 1.0000, with `g` exactly zero.**
Thresholding the gate closes the value channel completely and the address channel not at all, because
`k_r` is never multiplied by `g`. Only the flag touches it.

A prior-art review predicted "top-1 stays at or near 1.0000 and only the margin collapses". **That is
refuted**: the leak largely closes. But 0.0500 is 12.8× the chance rate of 0.0039, and mean rank 109
is below chance 127.5 — and at *n = 20* neither is resolvable (Clopper–Pearson 95 % CI
[0.0013, 0.2487], one-sided p = 0.075 against chance). At E-000028's own n = 500, a persisting 0.0500
would give CI [0.0326, 0.0729] at p = 1.1e-19, and the 2×-chance bar of 0.0079 sits at 4/500 with CI
[0.0022, 0.0204]. **So the single number that decides whether a gate can repair an address channel is
one this repository has never measured and can measure in 35 minutes.** That is Arm K.

**(b) At the gate value where the key channel provably does close, the certificate is unsound.** On
`e000010_seed0`, `hard_gate=True`, a SHRED-ed row, sweeping all 256 values of `bank["obj"]`:

```
gate on the shredded row: 0.0                       exactly zero: True
v_f numerically all-zero for all 256 payload values: True
distinct SIGN-BIT patterns over the 256 payload values: 256          <- a bijection
candidates matching the true payload's bit pattern: [91]   true obj: 91
so.audit._compare(0.0, -0.0) -> []    torch.equal -> True    int32-view equal -> False
```

and running the repository's own certifier on the repository's own repair, two SHRED-ed rows, all 255
alternative payloads each:

```
hard=True keygate=False | current (a-b).abs().max() -> certified=False  first=encode_bank[k_r] d=4.406e+00
hard=True keygate=False | bitwise int-view          -> certified=False  first=encode_bank[v_f] d=0.000e+00
hard=True keygate=True  | current (a-b).abs().max() -> certified=True   evals=511
hard=True keygate=True  | bitwise int-view          -> certified=False  first=encode_bank[v_f] d=0.000e+00
```

**`certify_encoding` issues CERTIFIED on the exact configuration proposed as the fix for E-000028, and
the shredded object is recoverable from the certified tensor, uniquely and exactly, at top-1.** Not an
attack that succeeded — an instrument that reports *no difference* where a difference exists, in the
sound-unsafe direction. It is the fourth instance of one shape after §31.10, §31.13 and §31.15: an
instrument that agrees with its specification on the distribution it was checked against and diverges
on the one the system occupies. Here the divergence opens *only when the gate is exactly shut* — which
is the only condition under which a certificate is ever issued.

**Two things I explicitly do not claim.** The sign-bit channel is not a new practical attack: an
adversary who can call `encode_bank` already holds `bank["obj"]`, and the pattern is annihilated by
the very next operation (measured: the sign of a zero does not survive `p @ v_f`). And **no recorded
verdict moves.** E-000030's synthetic arms run at `hard_gate=False` (`e000030…py:110`), where the
difference is numerically real and already recorded as a failure; the GPT-2 adapter computes
`values = payload·g + unk·(1−g)` (`so/llm_adapter.py:240`) and adding a nonzero `unk` destroys the sign of the zero (measured:
1 distinct bit pattern over 256 payloads, against 256 for the synthetic `v_f = v_f · g`), so §31.14's
certified rows stand. What changes is what "certified" is permitted to mean from here.

**The honest headline is therefore about instruments, not architecture**, and it corrects the
hypothesis's own framing. "Both failures are the same failure — a learned component asked to enforce a
specification enforced something else" is wrong about E-000028: `k_rev` is an addressing function and
was never asked to enforce anything. The correct unifying statement is *amplitude was mistaken for
support*, and the cost of the mistake is paid twice — once by the system at `g > 0`, once by the
auditor at `g == 0`.

## 2. The design chosen, and why it survives the refutations

**A named graft: `construct-not-learn` ∧ `exhaustive-cert`, executed as a measurement of the repair
already in the tree rather than as new architecture.** I take the thesis of the first — *deletion is
removal from the addressable set, decided by the store's Python and not by a learned score* — and the
instrument of the second — *a scoped bit-exact sweep with the numeric regime stamped on it* — and I
build neither of their mechanisms. That is not evasion; it is the direct consequence of reading the
surviving refutations, **every one of which was a refutation of new plumbing.**

**`index-cells`** died three times, all on machinery this design does not write: "the pointer it must
read is placed in the one value channel a reverse query never selects … the design therefore destroys
the capability instead of relocating it" (measured `read_norm 0.0000`); "`_reindex` has to fire
correctly on ten operations and on every interleaving of them"; and "THE CERTIFICATE'S COUNTERFACTUAL
STOPS BEING STORE-REALISABLE, AND IT IS THIS DESIGN THAT BREAKS IT". No new cell kind, no cascade, no
`_reindex` here.

**`no-reverse`** died on `content_free` banks — "**Blanking is upstream of the injection point** … the
sweep overwrites the blanked value", so its central arm "is unsatisfiable at any budget" — and on a
change that "destroys a pre-registered, recorded alias criterion … `delete_target/alias_unknown
1.0000 → 0.0000`". This design touches no bank builder. In particular `so/data.py:bank_from_store`
keeps its dead `respect_markers` parameter: four of its five call sites are inside the recorded
E-000024, and honouring it would set `active=False` on shredded rows, force `g` to zero from the
control plane, and silently convert that run's *learned* refusal into a mask — destroying the
comparison that makes its relearn attack meaningful.

**`construct-not-learn`** died on `readable_bank()` plumbing ("`_compare` … only admit
`torch.is_tensor` values … Zero violations is what the function returns before it looks at
anything"), and its third refutation hands this design its conclusion verbatim: "**`evict()` already
delivers the design's mechanism and its measured leak numbers, on unmodified checkpoints, with the
payload retained and RESTORE working** … the ~2 CPU-hours of retraining is spent re-deriving a result
the repository has." Adopted. EVICT is the answer; this experiment measures precisely why the
alternatives are not, and what a certificate may say about either.

**`exhaustive-cert`** is the design whose refutations I keep **as results rather than repair away**:

- *Fatality 1* — "`v_f` is numerically all-zero, yet its sign-bit pattern takes 256 DISTINCT values …
  Only a bit view sees it" — is Arm C's headline, now reproduced independently above. Its proposed
  repair (mask the payload before the embedding lookup) is **rejected**: it makes payload masking a
  function of the store's marker predicate applied inside the model, which is `no-reverse`'s A2 defect
  one layer along and contradicts `so/mvcc.py:385-391`'s documented contract that "the neural model has to
  learn to reject unsigned payloads itself". It is also unnecessary once the answer is EVICT.
- *Fatality 2* — "the repair destroys REVERSE-DIRECTION REFUSAL, and the pre-registered retention
  battery is constructed so it cannot see the loss … the `reverse` family … 1.0000 off AND on" —
  becomes criterion **R6**, split by whether the reference answer is UNKNOWN, the only split that can
  see it, plus **R7** exact-locality.
- *Fatality 3* — "`train_or_load` … DISCARDS the caller's `model_cfg` on the load path" so the flag
  cannot be toggled, and "register `shred_rev_key` unconditionally" would break all seven strict
  loaders — becomes code change **#3**, a local loader with a one-key whitelist assert. Already run:
  `missing == ["shred_rev_key"]`, `unexpected == []`, `n_params` 616451 → 616579.
- Its own weakest point — that `shred_rev_key` is an **untrained, unrecorded** parameter, so "two runs
  of the same experiment can give different numbers and the record cannot be re-scored" — becomes
  criterion **K8** (three draws, spread reported, seed stamped) and arm **K-null** (`shred_rev_key`
  tied to the *trained* `null_key[1]`, which already means "nothing found").

**Deliberately not fixed here, so a reader does not have to find it.** The marker gate's boundary
(§31.12: operational radius 0.90 against a declared 0.35, centre recoverable to 0.0061) is untouched
and its shell curve is re-reported unchanged, so this experiment cannot be credited with a fix it did
not make. And `certify_encoding` returns `Certificate(ok, ok, …)`, labelling an *encoding-only*
comparison as `activation_certified`, whose docstring promises "every tensor every submodule emitted".
That mislabel is real, but `so.audit.certify_fact` reads the field (`record_ok = record.output_certified
and record.activation_certified`) and E-000032's recorded fact certificates depend on it — so it is
**recorded as a known defect for a separate sitting, not silently changed**, and no statement in §31.19
may lean on it.

## 3. Code changes, in order

Small, ordered, one sitting. No default changes, no checkpoint is written, `so/model.py`,
`so/mvcc.py`, `so/data.py` and `so/train.py` are untouched.

1. **`so/audit.py::_compare` — make it bitwise.** In the floating branch, when `atol == 0.0`, view both
   tensors as `int32`/`int64` of matching width and compare with `torch.equal`; report
   `(a-b).abs().max()` as the magnitude so existing summaries read unchanged. Keep the magnitude test
   when `atol > 0` (no caller passes one, but a tolerance means a magnitude). Two holes close for
   free: signed zeros, and NaN — today `_compare([("x", tensor([nan]))], [("x", tensor([0.0]))], 0.0)`
   returns `[]`, because `NaN > 0.0` is `False`.
2. **`so/audit.py::Violation` and `::Difference` — add `bitwise_only: bool = False`,** set when the
   magnitude is `0.0` and the encodings differ; `__str__` renders "differs in IEEE encoding only
   (magnitude 0.0)". Without it the new violations read as nonsense.
3. **`so/experiments/e000001b_mini_transformer.py::load_variant(name, seed, expect_missing=(), **overrides)`
   — NEW, ~14 lines.** Load the checkpoint, build `ModelConfig(**ck["model_config"])`, apply overrides,
   `torch.manual_seed(VARIANT_SEED + seed)`, construct, `load_state_dict(strict=False)`, then
   `assert sorted(missing) == sorted(expect_missing) and not unexpected`. Returns model, centre, and a
   `stamp` (checkpoint sha256, overrides, variant seed, torch version, thread count, dtype).
   `train_or_load` is untouched, so all seven strict loaders keep working.
4. **`so/experiments/e000029_marker_geometry.py` — add `shell` to `__all__`.** Body untouched. Arm K
   dials the gate with it: `shell()` places a marker at an exact distance by construction, which
   rejection sampling cannot do.
5. **`so/experiments/e000036_gate_is_not_deletion.py` — NEW, the experiment.** Reuses
   `e000028_key_channel.{routing_of, locate_columns, sweep, score}` and `e000029_marker_geometry.shell`
   verbatim. Adds `bit_patterns(tensor, row, field, n_values)` → `(n_distinct, top1_recovery)` and
   `clopper_pearson(k, n)` + `binom_sf(k, n, p)` (stdlib `math.comb` only; `scipy` is not installed).
   Writes via `so.ledger.save`.
   *Methodological note that must appear in the file's docstring:* to dial `g`, the experiment writes
   `cell.version_obj(cell.active_version).marker = shell(rng, centre, d, 1)[0]` **directly**, rather
   than calling `shred()`. That keeps `so/mvcc.py` untouched, but it bypasses `_record`, so the store's
   log no longer replays to the state scored — acceptable here because the experiment trains nothing
   and persists no store, and it is the only way to reach shells inside 0.7 at all. Shells `< 0.7` are
   states `new_invalid_marker` cannot produce; they are the dial, and **criterion K5 restricts the
   verdict to shells ≥ 0.7**, which the store can.
6. **`so/tests/test_audit.py` — three tests.** (a) `_compare` reports a difference for `0.0` vs `-0.0`
   and none for identical tensors; (b) a hard-gated `encode_bank` over a 32-value domain yields 32
   distinct `v_f` bit patterns and `certify_encoding` refuses; (c) the adapter shape `payload*0 + unk`
   yields exactly one pattern — the immunity that keeps §31.14 standing, asserted rather than assumed.
7. **`docs/so-experiment-ledger.md` — new §31.19.** Records the comparator defect and that no recorded
   verdict moved; the `g`-curve and the verdict on `gate_reverse_key`; the correction to §31.10 point 4
   (under a hard gate the flag needs no training run); and the prior-art attribution from §1.

## 5. The soundness claim, stated to survive a hostile reviewer

**Claim.** Fix parameters θ, a bank `B` of `C` rows, a deleted row set `D`, payload fields `F` with
finite domain `V`, and the consumed interface `K = {k_f, v_f, k_r, v_r, active}` (synthetic;
`so/model.py:266-268`) or `{keys, values, active}` (adapter; `so/llm_adapter.py:328`). For each
`r ∈ D` and **each** `p ∈ V` — every value, not a sample — with all other rows held at their stored
values, `encode_bank(B[r ← p])` restricted to `K` is **bit-identical** to `encode_bank(B)`: identical
IEEE-754 encodings, hence identical including the sign of a zero and the payload of a NaN. Because
`forward` reads the bank in exactly one place and thereafter touches only `K`, the query tensors and
its own parameters — a premise `check_mediation` exists to *falsify*, not to assume — every downstream
quantity is identical **for every query**: any hop count, either mode, any phrasing, any attack not yet
written. As realised in float32 by the stamped torch build, at bank shape `C`, with the stamped thread
count.

**That is the sentence the current comparator does not support.** After change #1 it does. Before it,
the true statement was "numerically equal", and this experiment exhibits a case where the two differ
and the difference is a bijection onto the payload domain.

**Eight limits. A certificate that hides them is worse than none.**

1. **Marginal, not joint.** Rows are swept one at a time with the others held at their stored values.
   Joint dependence over `|V|^{|D|}` assignments is covered by `joint_trials` random draws, which are a
   sample. `joint_certified` says so; this experiment does not change it.
2. **A field of a row — not a fact.** Duplicates, aliases and derivations reach the same object through
   rows the sweep holds fixed. This repository has the extreme case on record:
   `dependency/derivable_recovery_after_revoke_K3 = 1.0` in every seed of E-000019 — a certified record
   deletion under which every derivable fact survives. `so.audit.certify_fact` composed with
   `so.closure` is what closes that gap. **A record certificate quoted as a fact certificate is the one
   misreading this document forbids.**
3. **The model, not the system.** `shred()` writes only `Version.marker`; `revoke()` only `status`;
   `store.log` retains the original `("write", {"obj": …})`. Anyone with store access does not need the
   model. Only `delete()` clears `versions`, and `evict()` deliberately does not.
4. **Existence is disclosed by design, not by oversight.** `k_f = k_fwd(LN(s + r))` is a function of the
   question, and must remain so — the model has to route onto a shredded row in order to refuse it.
   Only leaving the bank closes existence, and E-000028 measures what an attacker gets from the shape
   change alone: 0.0040 against 0.0039.
5. **Not portable across the numeric regime.** Bit-identity is a property of this build (torch
   2.14.0+cpu, 4 threads, this BLAS), established empirically by C8, not proved. A different backend
   may break it benignly — misreported as a leak — or round away a genuine 1-ULP dependence —
   misreported as a certificate. **I have no test for the second direction, and it is the dangerous one.**
6. **Exhaustiveness is a property of the toy.** `|V| = 256` single-token entities. For a text payload
   the sweep does not degrade, it dies. The domain-free form is `check_absence`'s membership argument —
   a payload with no bank row is not an input, over any domain, for every query — which is exactly why
   EVICT is the recommendation and the sweep is the falsifier, not the certificate.
7. **Method, not discovery.** This is self-composition for non-interference, decided by bounded
   enumeration of a finite secret domain. §31.19 will say so and cite it. What is ours is the object it
   is applied to and the defect found in our own implementation.
8. **Still not an activation-level claim.** `certify_encoding` compares the *encoding* and returns that
   verdict in `activation_certified` too. Left in place deliberately (§2), recorded as a known defect;
   no §31.19 statement may lean on it.

## 6. CPU cost, and what is deliberately not run

**Measured while writing this, on a contended box** — `nproc` 4, load average 28–32, another agent's
1.1 GB job resident. One E-000028-shaped attack cell (20 targets × 256 candidates × 2 conditions) took
**274–338 s**; the recorded idle rate for the same experiment is **64–79 s/seed for 100 targets × 5
conditions** (`e000028_key_channel.json`), ≈ 13× faster. Budgets use the *recorded* rate; contention is
schedule risk, and the contingency is 3 seeds instead of 5, which weakens only K5's CI.

| arm | work | cost |
|---|---|---|
| K1–K5 | 5 seeds × 8 shells × 2 flag settings × 100 targets, + live and evict controls | ≈ 14 s/cell × 80 + controls ≈ **35 min** |
| K7 | hard-gate endpoints, 5 seeds × 2 | ≈ 3 min |
| K8, K9 | 2 extra `shred_rev_key` draws + the null-tied arm, at the 3 decision shells only | ≈ **15 min** |
| C1 | E-000030 re-run, 3 synthetic seeds + GPT-2 seed 0, new comparator | 56–67 s/seed + 55 s ≈ **5 min** |
| C2–C5, C8 | 255 encodings × 3 configs × 5 seeds; adapter bit-patterns; determinism repeats | ≈ **20 min** |
| R1–R7 | `run_suite` on 5 checkpoints × 2 flag settings, + the reverse-refusal split at 3 shred fractions | ≈ **35 min** |
| R8 | bitwise identity of `k_r`/`v_f` on live rows, 5 seeds | seconds |
| **total** | | **≈ 1.9 CPU-hours idle; ~6–8 h at today's load** |

**Training runs: zero.** Under `hard_gate` the blend is bit-exactly the identity on live rows
(criterion R8), so the flag is a drop-in on the recorded E-000010 checkpoints; under the soft gate it
perturbs live keys slightly, and any capability cost that shows is a cost of the flag, not of training
— which is what R1–R7 are for. No checkpoint is written, so `guard_recorded_checkpoint` is never
tested and no record loses its reproducibility.

**Deliberately not run, so nothing is silently promised.**

- **Any GPT-2 training** (20–90 min/seed). The adapter's key is `k_proj(ln_key(subj + rel))` with no
  object in it, so E-000028's channel cannot exist there; the GPT-2 work here is encoding-only (C1, C2)
  and costs minutes.
- **The joint sweep** over more than one deleted row: `256^k`. Limit 1 stands rather than being papered
  over with more `joint_trials`.
- **Retraining the gate on the 0.35–0.7 annulus**, which §31.12 prescribes. That is the fix for a
  different defect (5 training runs plus a full re-measurement of the shell curve), and crediting this
  experiment with it would be exactly the conflation §31.13 was written about. E-000029's curve is
  re-reported unchanged.
- **Any architectural change**: no link-index cells, no `readable_bank()`/`projected_bank`, no
  `content_free` banks, no `use_reverse=False`, no masking before the embedding lookup, no change to
  `bank_from_store`. §2 gives the refutation that kills each.
- **Fixing `certify_encoding`'s `activation_certified` mislabel** — E-000032's recorded fact
  certificates read that field. Recorded as a defect, scheduled separately.
- **A `(subject, relation)` sweep** beyond one demonstration row: it is expected to fail for SHRED and
  REVOKE and to pass structurally for EVICT/DELETE. That is limit 4, not a result.
- **Any claim at LLM scale, any model above 124M, any multi-token payload.**
